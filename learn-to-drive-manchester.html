What You Need Before You Start

Three things, and only one takes effort. You must be at least 17 (16 if you receive the enhanced rate of PIP for mobility), able to read a standard number plate from 20 metres — with glasses or contact lenses if you need them — and hold a provisional driving licence. Apply for the provisional on GOV.UK; it normally arrives within a week and costs £34 online. You don't need your own car, insurance or any experience: lessons are in our dual-controlled car, and insurance is included.

How Learning to Drive Works: First Lesson to Pass

Learning to drive is a sequence, not a scramble. Your first lesson covers the cockpit drill — seat, mirrors, seatbelt — and moving off and stopping safely on a quiet street in your area. From there, lessons build one skill at a time: junctions and roundabouts, meeting traffic and parked cars, then dual carriageways, independent sat-nav driving, night and wet-weather driving, and every manoeuvre the examiner can ask for. Before your test we run mock tests under exam conditions around your local centre, so test day holds no surprises. Not sure how long all that takes? Our how many driving lessons do you need guide uses DVSA averages to give you a realistic number.

Learning to Drive in Manchester — FAQs

What do I need before I can learn to drive in Manchester?

A provisional driving licence, to be at least 17 (16 on the enhanced rate of PIP for mobility), and eyesight good enough to read a number plate from 20 metres. The provisional comes from GOV.UK and usually arrives within a week.

How many lessons does a beginner need?

DVSA's averages are around 45 hours of lessons plus 22 hours of private practice, but beginners here typically need 20–40 hours. You'll get an honest estimate after your first lesson — see how many lessons you'll need.

Are beginner lessons different from refresher lessons?

Yes — everything starts from zero: cockpit drill, moving off and stopping on quiet streets, then one new skill per stage. Nothing is assumed and nothing is rushed.

Do you teach nervous drivers?

All the time. Lessons are one-to-one at your pace, starting on quiet roads until you feel steady — and there's no such thing as a silly question.
